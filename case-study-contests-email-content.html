The Problem

A Radio Audience Disappears Between Spots

A station knows how many people listen, but it cannot reach any of them by name. When a spot ends, so does the contact. An advertiser who wants a second place to reach the same local people has nowhere to go.

A daily email is that second place. But a list only works if the people on it asked to be there. A list that is bought, or filled in without asking, is full of strangers who never open it. We wanted a list of neighbors who chose to join.

What We Did

One Unchecked Box, on Every Entry Form

Our stations run on-air contests all year. Every entry form for those contests has one extra line at the bottom: “Sign me up for the Finger Lakes Daily News email.”

☑
The box starts empty
Nobody is signed up unless they tick it themselves. We never turn it on for them, and we never add a person who entered a contest without ticking it.
Opt-in only
🎧
The radio does the promoting
The station talks up the prize on air. Listeners go to the entry page. The email signup is one tick away, at the moment they are already handing over their name.
Radio feeds the list
📊
Every entry is counted
We record each entry by contest and by prize, and whether the box was ticked. That shows which giveaways bring in subscribers and which bring in only entries.
Tracked by contest
Why not just add everyone? A person who asked for the email opens it. A person who did not ask marks it as spam, and that hurts delivery for the whole list. The unchecked box costs us some signups. It keeps the list worth advertising to.

The Results

The List Grew Fastest in the Months With a Big Giveaway

Since the first contest we can count (late March), there have been 5,182 contest entries. On 1,861 of them, which is 36%, the person ticked the email box. The left chart shows box ticks by month. The right chart shows the size of the email list at the end of each month.

A big prize adds a burst

The cabin giveaway drew 278 entries on its first day, and 124 of them ticked the box. The three giveaways that drew more than 580 entries each brought in 224 to 244 box ticks. Smaller giveaways, like tickets to one show, brought in 4 to 70.

An always-open drawing sets the floor

One gift-card drawing stayed open from March to now and collected between 75 and 136 box ticks in every full month. In July, when no new giveaway ran, all of the month’s 76 box ticks came from the two drawings that were already open.

What the numbers do and do not say. The 1,861 box ticks came from 852 different people, because people enter more than once and some were already on the list. The email also collects signups from the news site, so we do not credit every new subscriber to a contest. What we can say is that the list grew by 2,093 subscribers (70%) from the end of February to Nov 19, and that it grew most in the months with a big giveaway.
📬
The people who joined read it. Over the last 31 daily sends (Oct 21 to Nov 19), about 45% of subscribers opened each email. Some of those opens are automatic ones from phone privacy settings, so the true figure is lower, but the list is awake.

What It Means for Advertisers

Radio Builds the Audience, the Email Keeps It

An advertiser on our stations is not buying a spot and hoping. The same contest that gets a listener to the entry page also puts a local person on a list that gets a message every morning, with a link you can click and a count of who clicked.

The method is not specific to us. Any business that runs a giveaway can put the same unchecked box on its own entry form. A radio contest that sends people to that form turns a one-week promotion into a list of customers who asked to hear from you again.
